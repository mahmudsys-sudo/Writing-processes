# Writing-processes

This is an excellent topic for MSc students, early-career researchers, public health professionals, IT professionals, and academic staff because secondary research, evidence synthesis, and review articles are increasingly accepted by journals and often require fewer resources than primary data collection.

# 1. Types of Review Articles and Secondary Research Publications

## A. Traditional Literature Review

Purpose:

* Summarize existing knowledge
* Identify trends and gaps

Process:

1. Define topic
2. Search literature
3. Read and summarize
4. Organize themes
5. Discuss findings

Suitable for:

* New researchers
* MSc dissertations
* Emerging technologies

---

## B. Narrative Review

Purpose:

* Broad overview of a subject
* Expert interpretation

Example Topics:

* AI in Public Health
* Cloud Adoption in Universities
* ERP Implementation Challenges

Process:

1. Topic selection
2. Literature search
3. Theme development
4. Critical discussion

Advantages:

* Easier than systematic review
* Good publication opportunity

---

## C. Systematic Review

Purpose:

* Answer a specific research question

Uses:

* Healthcare
* Public Health
* Health Informatics
* Cybersecurity

Framework:

PICO

Population
Intervention
Comparison
Outcome

Process:

1. Protocol development
2. Search strategy
3. Database search
4. Screening
5. Quality assessment
6. Data extraction
7. Synthesis
8. PRISMA reporting

---

## D. Scoping Review

Purpose:

* Map existing evidence
* Identify gaps

Good for:

* Health Informatics
* Research Data Management
* AI Governance
* Digital Health

Process:

1. Define objectives
2. Search literature
3. Categorize studies
4. Identify research gaps

---

## E. Rapid Review

Purpose:

* Fast evidence synthesis

Used by:

* WHO
* Government agencies
* NGOs

Time:

* 1–3 months

---

## F. Meta-Analysis

Purpose:

* Statistical combination of multiple studies

Requirements:

* Systematic review completed first

Tools:

* R
* RevMan
* Stata

---

## G. Umbrella Review

Purpose:

* Review of systematic reviews

Highest level evidence.

---

## H. Integrative Review

Purpose:

* Combine qualitative and quantitative studies

Common in:

* Education
* Nursing
* Public Health

---

## I. Critical Review

Purpose:

* Critically evaluate existing knowledge

Suitable for:

* Systems Thinking
* Project Management
* IT Governance

---

## J. Bibliometric Analysis

Purpose:

* Analyze publication trends

Tools:

* VOSviewer
* Biblioshiny
* Scopus

Example:
"Global Research Trends in AI-Based Disease Surveillance"

---

## K. Mapping Review

Purpose:

* Map research areas and gaps

Useful for:

* Health Systems
* ERP
* Cybersecurity

---

## L. Systematic Mapping Study

Common in Software Engineering.

Useful Topics:

* MLOps
* Cloud Computing
* ERP
* Network Security

# My recommendation for your institution

For a very practical publication strategy is:

**Level 1 (Beginner):**

* Narrative Reviews
* Critical Reviews
* KAP Study Reviews

**Level 2 (Intermediate):**

* Scoping Reviews
* Systematic Mapping Studies
* Bibliometric Analyses

**Level 3 (Advanced):**

* Systematic Reviews
* Meta-Analyses
* Umbrella Reviews

This progression allows MSc students to publish within 3–12 months while developing the skills needed for doctoral-level research and international journal publication.
